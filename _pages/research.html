--- layout: single title: "Research" permalink: /research/ author_profile: true --- ## Current PhD Project — Spotted Hyena Socio-Spatial Behavior *Michigan State University · Eli Strauss Lab · 2024–present* I am currently studying the **socio-spatial behavior of wild spotted hyenas (*Crocuta crocuta*)** in response to demographic changes and increasing anthropogenic pressure. My research is based at the **Maasai Mara National Reserve, Kenya**, where I spent 2025 collecting long-term behavioral and spatial data in the field. Spotted hyenas are highly social carnivores whose group dynamics and space use are shaped by complex social hierarchies, competition, and environmental pressures. By integrating behavioral observations with spatial analysis, I aim to understand how hyena clans respond to shifts in group composition and human activity at the reserve boundaries. --- ## M.Sc. Project — Neonatal Growth of Harbour Seals *Université de Sherbrooke · Pelletier Lab · 2022–2024* My master's research characterized **neonatal growth patterns in harbour seal pups (*Phoca vitulina*)** living in the St. Lawrence Estuary, Canada, under the supervision of [Prof. Fanie Pelletier](https://labopelletier.weebly.com/) and [Dr. Xavier Bordeleau](https://profils-profiles.science.gc.ca/fr/profil/xavier-bordeleau). The project had two main objectives: **1. Age estimation using umbilical cord degeneration** I validated a non-invasive method for estimating pup age based on umbilical cord degeneration stage — a practical tool for field researchers working with newborn phocids where disturbance must be minimized. **2. Neonatal growth patterns and their drivers** Using repeated mass measurements across the nursing period, I modeled growth trajectories and evaluated the role of individual, behavioral, and environmental factors on pup growth. Key findings: - Growth followed a **non-linear Gompertz-type curve**, consistent with the rapid mass gain typical of phocid seals during the short nursing period - **No sex differences** in growth pattern were detected, though males were on average ~4% heavier at birth - A **silver spoon effect** was found: pups born heavier grew faster, suggesting early condition predicts short-term performance - No strong environmental correlates of growth were identified over the study period --- ## Undergraduate Research — Age at Primiparity in Eastern Grey Kangaroos *Wilsons Promontory National Park, Australia* As an undergraduate, I worked on a project examining the **long-term consequences of age at first reproduction (primiparity) in eastern grey kangaroos (*Macropus giganteus*)** using a long-term individual monitoring dataset from Wilsons Promontory National Park, Victoria, Australia. Using **Bayesian regression models**, I explored the life-history trade-offs associated with early vs. late age at primiparity. Results suggested that females reproducing earlier experienced reduced somatic growth, but showed greater overall reproductive success across their lifetime — consistent with classic life-history theory predictions under variable resource environments.
